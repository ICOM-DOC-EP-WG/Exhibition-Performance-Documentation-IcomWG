### Introduction

My name is Zoë Renaudie. I am a contemporary art conservator, trained in France.

I previously worked at the Collection Lambert, a contemporary art center in Avignon, where I was responsible for conservation-related issues across the collection and exhibitions. My work involved close collaboration with curators, registrars, technicians and artists, and included the management of collections documentation, archives, and conservation aspects of exhibition production.

I later joined LUMA Arles as Registrar and Conservator for so-called “complex works”, where I focused in particular on the production, installation, and documentation of works involving performance, time-based media, or protocol-based elements. In this context, documentation was not only a record but a working tool, closely connected to decision-making, care, and long-term preservation.

In both institutions, I participated in the development and implementation of digital documentation tools and data structures, responding to concrete institutional needs rather than predefined standards. This experience allowed me to better understand the gaps between existing models and everyday museum practices.

Through these roles, I worked extensively on the documentation of exhibitions and performances, both at a practical level and within broader institutional frameworks, which progressively led me to question documentation as a methodological and epistemological issue, rather than a purely technical one. I decided to consolidate these reflections into a doctoral research project, which I am currently pursuing at the Université de Montréal, under the supervision of Emmanuel Château-Dutier. 

Within this framework, I wanted to join this research group in order to share my work with other professionals and researchers, and to contribute to a collective reflection grounded in practice. When I contacted the group last year, Alexandre Matos was looking for someone to take the lead, so here we are !

### Background of the Working Group

Of what I know, this working group was originally founded by Gabriel Moore Forell Bevilacqua, whose work focused on documentation systems, performance conservation, and artists’ archives in art museums at the Sao Paolo University. He served as Vice-President of CIDOC (ICOM’s International Committee for Documentation) from 2013 to 2019, where he coordinated the Working Group on Performance Documentation. At some point, exhibition documentation was added to the scope of the group, as it appeared closely connected to performance documentation practices. Unfortunately, most of the previous working materials and outputs have been lost. This loss is precisely what motivates my interest in documenting not only exhibitions and performances, but also our own working processes and methodologies.

### Objectives

My primary objective is to rebuild and reactivate this research group, and to create a network between academics and museum professionals working on documentation, conservation, and data models related to exhibitions and performances.

Ideally, over the coming years, the group could work toward the following milestones in order to support and gradually fulfill its mandate :

#### 1. Bibliography & Project Mapping

\- Create and publish a shared Zotero bibliography on the documentation of exhibitions and performances.

\- Compile a list of relevant projects in museums and universities in order to map our network.

\- Develop conceptual definitions of exhibitions (in-person and digital) and performance, to clearly delimit the working group’s scope.

#### 2. Definitions, Questionnaires, and Data Models

\- Design an institutional diagnostic questionnaire to better understand current documentation practices and expand our network map.

\- Present and analyse the results of the questionnaire.

\- Conduct a metadata crosswalk, comparing existing standards and models (Spectrum, CDWA, CCO, CONA, LIDO, CIDOC-CRM, CIDOC Guidelines, etc.).

\- Identify data models specifically adapted to documenting exhibitions and performances.

#### 3. Documentation Strategies and Event Planning

\- Share documentation strategies for preserving exhibitions and performances and ensuring future access.

\- Organise a working group event to present our findings and collectively define new objectives.

### A Realistic and Flexible Approach

That said, all of this will naturally depend on the energy and availability of the group.

This is voluntary work, and the goal is not to exhaust ourselves.

Some ICOM working groups meet weekly; others meet once a year.

Given that we are a very international group, I suggest working through task forces, which can meet independently and at their own rhythm.

### Focus for the Coming Year

For this year, I suggest that we focus on the first phase: Bibliography & Project Mapping.

Concretely, this would involve:

\- Establishing clear communication channels.

\- Creating a GitHub or GitLab platform to document our work and publish results.

\- Creating a task force dedicated to the Zotero bibliography.

\- Creating a task force to list and map ongoing projects.

\- Beginning to collect definitions, key concepts, and references.

\- And, for those who are interested, proposing one or more articles for the new ICOM journal on documentation.

**Points raised by the different groups and individuals**

- **Is performance still an important part of this group?**
  Yes. There is a mix of specialists in both exhibition and performance. It is interesting to see how the documentation of exhibitions and performance art intersects and informs each other.

- **Inviting other profiles (e.g. media art)**
  It was suggested to invite participants from media art, as they face similar documentation issues. The group already includes members from ICOFOM and ICOM Exhibition. We should also reach out to the ICOM-CC Documentation Working Group.

- **Questionnaires on access to exhibitions and performance**
  This is planned for the second phase of the project, next year, once solid foundations are established.

- **Will CIDOC build databases?**
  No. As a working group, CIDOC provides guidelines but does not develop databases itself.
  For mapping existing projects, one option could be to use Wikidata as a shared database, alongside guidelines on how to contribute and structure the data.

- **Knowing who is working on what**
  It would be useful to identify who is working on which types of projects, in order to group participants around shared interests and potential case studies.
  A first step could be simple self-presentations. There is a clear demand for a discussion platform, which would be easier to structure once task groups are defined. The aim is not to separate exhibition and performance, but to work on them together. 

- **How often should we meet?**

  We agreed it would be easier for each sub task group to decide on that depending on what they want to accomplish for the year. 

- **Scope: museums only, or other spaces?**
  This is a key question that still needs to be addressed. Although the group operates within the Museum Council, it would be relevant to consider differences between institutional contexts (museums) and other environments of creation, such as artist-run or independent spaces.

- **Organisation** 
  Everybody is ok with the github platform to document and publish our work. We should make e-calendar events for meetings and create a dedicated email.
