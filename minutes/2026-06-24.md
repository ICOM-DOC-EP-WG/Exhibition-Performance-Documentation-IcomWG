## Presentation of Georgia Sharp's Research

Georgia Sharp previously worked with scenographic archives in a theatrical context and is currently researching this type of documentation. She expressed interest in contributing to the topic, though she is not currently affiliated with a scenographic archive or institution—she is conducting her work as an independent researcher.

Based in **São Paulo, Brazil**, Georgia Sharp is an architect and a master’s student in **Museum Studies** at the **Museum of Archaeology and Ethnology (MAE) at the University of São Paulo**. Her research focuses on **technical drawing as a tool for museological communication**, with a case study centered on the exhibitions at **Ipiranga Museum** in São Paulo. She primarily analyzes institutional documentation, particularly technical drawings produced for permanent exhibitions and related files.

This led her to question how institutions manage these processes and how the internal organization of exhibition archives supports research. She asked: *How can we organize these documents? How do we create a management system for them?* Since exhibitions often take years to develop, documenting their evolution is critical.

**Academic and Professional Background**

- **Education**: Master’s in Museum Studies (MAE, University of São Paulo).
- **Research Focus**: Technical drawing as a tool for museological communication.
- **Case Study**: Exhibitions at Ipiranga Museum.
- **Previous Experience**: Work with scenographic archives (theatrical context).
